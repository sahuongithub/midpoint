02
why nobody fixes it

One word in US securities law

“…any NMS security other than an option.” 17 CFR 242.600 — the definition of an NMS stock

Trade shares, and SEC Rule 605 forces your broker to publish an execution-quality receipt.

Trade options, and that rule does not apply. So no options execution data exists for anyone to compare against.

05
ai logic

One loop every 70 seconds. Every stage can only say no.

01
Look
VIX term structure and the SPY option chain. Permission comes from the slope, not the level.
02
Build
One candidate: short strike near 0.20 delta, loss capped by a long leg one strike out.
03
Judge
Fourteen risk gates in fixed order. Any single one can veto the trade outright.
04
Act
Send only if all fourteen agree. Close at 50% of credit. Flat by 15:15 ET.

The agent claims no forecasting edge. Its intelligence is in what it declines — and it declines 96% of what it considers.

Conventional transaction-cost analysis structurally cannot do this: it is built from executed orders and is blind to every trade never made. Storing the full market snapshot behind each refusal is what closes that gap.

08
profit and loss — honestly

$13 on $100,000. Here is why that is the number.

Over five sessions, a skilled strategy and a lucky one produce numbers you cannot tell apart. Grinold & Kahn: SE of an information ratio is 1/√years — ~16 years to establish top-quartile skill

So a big five-day P&L is not evidence of skill. It is evidence of variance.

  • 5 trades across 2 sessions
  • Every one closed at the agent's own target
  • No losing round trip
  • Every dollar attributed by the order that caused it
  • Research spending split out, never blended
  • A $0.30 gap traced to 12 legs at $0.025 — and recorded as a residual
